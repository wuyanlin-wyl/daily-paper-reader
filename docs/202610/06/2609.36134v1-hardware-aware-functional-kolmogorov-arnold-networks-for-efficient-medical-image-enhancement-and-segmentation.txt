# Hardware-Aware Functional Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks for Efficient Medical Image Enhancement and Segmentation 

Mohammad Sadegh Sirjani 1

> 1

Department of Computer Science University of Texas at San Antonio, San Antonio, TX, USA mohammadsadegh.sirjani@utsa.edu 

Abstract —Functional Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (FunKAN) achieve state-of-the-art accuracy on MRI Gibbs artifact removal and anatomical segmentation, but their 11.6 M parameters and 8.7 GFLOPs are too large for edge medical devices. We present FunKANLite, a two-stage, hardware-aware compression of FunKAN for point-of-care use. FunKANLite-TR reduces the spatial prior and replaces the ResBlock offset predictor with a depthwise-separable block. It has 1.9× fewer parameters than FunKAN and no loss in accuracy. We then distill FunKANLite-TR into FunKANLite-ST, which lowers the Hermite basis rank, factorizes the spatial prior into alow-rank form, and halves the filter widths. FunKANLite-ST has 5.6× fewer parameters and 3.7× fewer GFLOPs than FunKAN. It stays within 1.4 pp IoU of FunKAN on BUSI, GlaS, and CVC-ClinicDB, and reaches 33.95 dB PSNR on IXI. On an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano and a Raspberry Pi 5, FunKANLite-ST reduces energy per inference by up to 68% 

and raises throughput by 2.9×.

Index Terms —Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks, Medical Image Analysis, Knowledge Distillation, Edge Device. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Deploying diagnostic AI at the point of care requires models that are both accurate and compact. Two tasks are central to this goal: MRI Gibbs artifact removal, which reduces the k-space truncation ringing that blurs fine anatomical detail [1], [2], and medical image segmentation, which automatically outlines lesions and structures in oncology, gastroenterology, and radiology [3]–[9]. State-of-the-art models perform well on both tasks, but remain far too large for the memory and power limits of the edge hardware used in portable and bedside devices [10]–[20]. These high-performing models mostly rely on convolutional or transformer backbones whose components are designed mainly by experiment, with little theoretical basis [3], [21], [22]. Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) [23] offer amore theory-based alternative: learnable single-variable func-tions built on the Kolmogorov-Arnold representation the-orem [24], where orthogonal-polynomial variants such as ChebyKAN [25] and HermiteKAN [26] improve stability; medical variants such as U-KAN [27], UKAGNet [28], and Kolmogorov-Arnold Transformers [29] extend KAN back-bones to vision. A shared limitation of these models is that they treat the input as an unordered set of scalars, ignoring the 2D spatial structure of images. FunKAN [30] addresses 

Fig. 1: Overview of FunKANLite. The network processes medical images and runs on an edge device for MRI Gibbs artifact suppression and anatomical segmentation. this by extending the Kolmogorov-Arnold theorem to function spaces: it treats each 2D feature map as an element of a Hilbert space and builds its inner functions from a Fourier expansion over Hermite basis functions. This spatially-aware backbone achieves the best reported accuracy on MRI Gibbs removal (IXI [31]) and segmentation (BUSI [32], GlaS [33], CVC-ClinicDB [34]), but at 11.6 M parameters and 8.7 GFLOPs it remains too large for edge deployment. We present FunKANLite, a hardware-aware compression of FunKAN for edge deployment, shown in Fig. 1. FunKANLite-TR reduces the spatial prior from 145 × 145 to 110 × 110 

and replaces the ResBlock offset predictor with a depthwise-separable block. It has 1.9× fewer parameters than FunKAN and no drop in accuracy. We then apply knowledge distilla-tion [35]–[37] and train FunKANLite-ST from FunKANLite-TR. FunKANLite-ST lowers the Hermite basis rank, factorizes the spatial prior into a low-rank form, and halves the filter widths. It has 5.6× fewer parameters and 3.7× fewer GFLOPs than FunKAN. We measure both models on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano and a Raspberry Pi 5. Our contributions are:

A. Architecture Design 

We realize the two-stage plan from Section I. FunKANLite-TR slims the FunKAN operator without losing accuracy. FunKANLite-ST (Section II-B) is distilled from FunKANLite-TR and sized to the on-chip memory of the target hard-ware. Fig. 2 shows the architecture shared by FunKANLite-TR and FunKANLite-ST. We adopt FunKAN [30] as our baseline without changes, keeping its U-Net-style outer shell: a convolutional embedding, a stack of pre-activated residual encoder blocks [21], [38] (lifting), three FunKAN-operator backbone blocks, a symmetric residual decoder with skip connections (projection), and a 1 × 1 restoration head. For MRI enhancement, skip connections are removed and a 5×5

embedding is used. For segmentation, a 3 × 3 embedding and encoder-decoder skip connections are enabled. All of our changes stay inside the FunKAN operator block. The outer network remains unchanged.

2) Compact Spatial Prior: In FunKAN [30], the spatial prior ϕ is stored at 145 ×145 resolution and bilinearly interpo-lated to the current feature-map size (H × W ) at each forward pass. This resolution matches the MRI input and is larger than the compressed model needs. FunKANLite-TR stores the prior at 110 ×110 . This cuts the ϕ parameter count per backbone block by (145 2 − 110 2)/145 2 ≈ 42% . Runtime interpolation keeps the spatial adaptivity. Because ϕ is always interpolated before use, the attention computation in Eq. (1) is unchanged. 

3) Depthwise-Separable Offset Predictor: The ResBlock offset predictor in FunKAN carries two full 3×3 convolutions (c → c → 2c), costing O(18 c2) parameters and proportional MACs per backbone block. At the 128 -channel backbone stage used for segmentation, this accounts for a large share of the model size. FunKANLite-TR replaces the ResBlock with a depthwise-separable block [12]:
