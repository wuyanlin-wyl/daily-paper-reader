# Decouple, Purify and Unite: Semantic–Structural Prototype Learning for Federated Medical Segmentation 

Xingyue Zhao, Wenke Huang, Linghao Zhuang, Yanzhou Su, Zhifeng Wang, Haoyu Zhao, Mengfan Li, Junjun He, Tao Tan, Senior Member, IEEE, Dakai Jin, Le Lu, Fellow, IEEE, 

Mang Ye, Senior Member, IEEE, Qiang Yang, Fellow, IEEE, and Ming Feng 

Abstract —Federated learning enables medical institutions to train a global model without sharing data, yet feature heterogeneity from diverse scanners or protocols remains challenging. Existing representation-based methods face two limitations: 1) Incomplete Contextual Representation Learning: single-layer or coupled representations overlook multi-level structural cues and entangle regional semantics with boundary details. 2) Layerwise Style and Aggregation Biases: domain-specific style discrepancies across intermediate layers degrade prototypes, while aggregation that overlooks client distribution shifts can further amplify bias. We propose FedBCS+, federated decoupled contextual alignment with style-purified aggregation. We employ Frequency-domain Style Recalibration (FSR) in prototype construction to decouple content-style representations and extract style-purified prototypes. Built upon these purified features, Decoupled Contextual Prototype Alignment (DCPA) explicitly decouples multi-level features into semantic and structural prototypes and aligns regional semantics and fine-grained anatomical structures separately. Style-purified Semantic Prototype Aggregation (S2PA) measures each client’s purified prototype divergence from the global consensus and adaptively reweights aggregation toward under-represented clients to reduce consensus bias. On five heterogeneous medical segmentation benchmarks spanning histopathology, MRI, ultrasound, and colonoscopy, FedBCS+ achieves the highest mean Dice among the compared methods. A convergence analysis further characterizes how aggregation and alignment affect the optimization bound. 

Index Terms —Heterogeneous Federated Learning, Medical Image Segmentation, Prototype Learning, Feature Decoupling

Current FL ap-proaches, largely inheriting paradigms from classification tasks, typically construct prototypes solely from the deepest network bottlenecks. This deep-layer pooling compresses spatial dimensions, forcing both semantic and structural cues into a single, coupled representation. Aligning these coupled features across clients entangles internal semantics with boundary details and causes semantic–structural in-terference: pursuing a global semantic consensus tends to suppress the multi-level structural cues that dense segmen-tation requires [16], [17]. This raises the first question: (a) How to explicitly decouple the feature space to capture more comprehensive context? 

Beyond incomplete contextual representations, existing methods face another limitation spanning both local and server sides of the federated pipeline: Layerwise Style and Aggregation Biases . On the local client side, medical images exhibit significant feature heterogeneity due to diverse scan-ners and imaging protocols [18], [19]. Existing methods align final-layer representations [11], yet domain-specific style discrepancies remain pronounced in intermediate layers [20], [21]. This persistent layer-wise discrepancy degrades the quality of extracted prototypes before they are even uploaded. At the server, conventional parameter aggrega-tion weights client models by their local sample counts [2], without using prototype relationships to characterize dis-tribution shifts. Under data skews [22], [23], sample counts alone do not capture how style discrepancies affect local representations or how client distributions relate to one an-other. The resulting aggregation favors dominant domains and compounds the bias introduced during local feature extraction. A reliable global consensus therefore requires ag-gregation weights that account for the relationships among the uploaded prototypes. Together, the local style corruption and the global aggregation bias raise the second question: 

(b) How to extract style-purified prototypes locally and exploit their correlations globally to rectify the consensus bias? 

To address these challenges, we propose FedBCS+ , a federated medical segmentation framework that integrates style recalibration, decoupled prototype alignment, and prototype-guided parameter aggregation. As illustrated in Fig. 2, we first employ Frequency-domain Style Recalibra-tion (FSR) to adaptively modulate the amplitude spectra of intermediate features while preserving their phase infor-mation. This suppresses site-specific appearance variations before prototype construction and yields style-purified fea-tures . From these features, Decoupled Contextual Prototype Alignment (DCPA) integrates multiple encoder and decoder levels to capture both regional semantics and fine-grained anatomical structure. It constructs separate semantic and structural prototypes from region-center and boundary fea-tures and aligns the two types independently across clients. This decoupling reduces the interference caused by a shared prototype. On the server, Style-purified Semantic Prototype Ag-gregation (S2PA) uses the purified semantic and structural prototypes to guide model aggregation. It measures each client’s prototype divergence from the global consensus and converts this information into adaptive parameter aggre-gation weights. Clients whose representations are under-represented in the consensus receive greater weight, so their distributions are better reflected in the global model. The same prototypes thus serve both local alignment and server-side aggregation. A preliminary version of this work, FedBCS, was pub-lished at AAAI 2026 [24]. It introduced FSR and Context-aware Dual-level Prototype Alignment (CDPA), which com-bine style recalibration with hierarchical encoder–decoder feature fusion for federated segmentation. FedBCS+ retains FSR and the multi-level fusion design. The journal version extends FedBCS in four ways:

2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 Federated Learning with Heterogeneous Data 

Federated learning (FL) provides a privacy-preserving framework for collaborative model training without central-izing data [25]. FedAvg [2] establishes the basic framework by iteratively aggregating locally trained model parameters. Although effective in homogeneous settings, FedAvg strug-gles with data heterogeneity, which is common in medical imaging because differences in acquisition protocols, stain-ing procedures, and patient populations lead to substantial distribution shifts across institutions [26]. Existing methods address this challenge from several perspectives [3]. Some improve cross-domain performance [11], [27], [28], while others emphasize fairness across heterogeneous clients, in-cluding group fairness [29], collaboration fairness [18], and performance fairness [30], [31]. FedBN [32] retains local batch-normalization statistics to address feature shift, while FCCL+ [33] supports collaboration across heterogeneous model architectures. Domain generalization methods [19], [34] aim to generalize to unseen distributions. Our work focuses on learning a global segmentation model that gen-eralizes across heterogeneous medical imaging domains. 

2.2 Prototype-based Representation Alignment in Fed-erated Learning 

Many FL methods regularize local training to address data heterogeneity [35], [36], [37]; for example, MOON [8] uses contrastive learning to align local and global repre-sentations. Prototype-based approaches summarize same-class features as compact mean vectors, reducing the need to share individual feature representations. FedProto [10] minimizes the distance between local and global class prototypes. However, approaches developed primarily for label skew do not explicitly account for domain shift, limiting their cross-domain generalization. To address do-main skew, FPL [11] combines server-side prototype clus-tering with contrastive learning to align same-class repre-sentations, while using a single local prototype per class. FedContrast-GPA [38] and FedPLVM [12] cluster client fea-tures to produce multiple prototypes per class, whereas FedGMKD [13] constructs prototypes using Gaussian mix-ture models. These methods nevertheless rely on final-layer encoder features, overlooking both the influence of domain-specific style on prototype quality and the multi-level con-text required for dense segmentation. Our method con-structs style-purified prototypes through frequency-domain recalibration and explicitly decouples semantic and struc-tural representations for fine-grained alignment. 

2.3 Federated Aggregation Strategies 

FedAvg [2] aggregates client parameters with weights pro-portional to local dataset sizes. Under data heterogeneity, weighting solely by dataset size does not account for dif-ferences in client distributions and can yield suboptimal global models [22]. Several approaches improve federated optimization and aggregation. FedProx [9] adds a proxi-mal term to limit local model drift, and SCAFFOLD [39] introduces control variates to correct drift during local updates. FedNova [40] normalizes local updates to ad-dress objective inconsistency caused by different numbers of local training steps. Beyond parameter-level corrections, distillation-based methods such as FedDF [41] aggregate models by distilling ensemble predictions on a shared unla-beled dataset. For medical FL, FedContrast-GPA [38] adjusts aggregation weights according to client training dynamics. FedGMKD [13] uses discrepancy-aware aggregation to ac-count for distribution differences. These strategies motivate incorporating the semantic content of client representations into aggregation without requiring auxiliary public data. To this end, S2PA measures the divergence between style-purified client prototypes and the global consensus, and converts these measurements into adaptive parameter ag-gregation weights. 

2.4 Feature Decoupling for Dense Segmentation 

Dense segmentation relies on region-level semantics for tar-get identification and structural cues for precise boundary delineation. Prior work shows that combining these cues in a single representation creates conflicting optimization pressures [16]. DecoupleNet [42] studies feature decou-pling for domain-adaptive segmentation, while Decoupled Semantic Prototypes [43] support learning from diverse annotation types in semi-weakly supervised segmentation. The semantic–structural distinction is particularly relevant to medical imaging: regional semantics identify organs or lesions, while structural cues delineate anatomical bound-aries, with both varying substantially across MRI, CT, and histology [14], [44]. In prototype-based FL, coupling these cues within shared prototypes introduces an additional challenge. Aggregating coupled prototypes from clients with different imaging protocols smooths both semantic and structural information, causing their alignment objectives to interfere across communication rounds. DCPA addresses this interference by constructing separate semantic and structural prototypes and aligning them independently.

3.1 Frequency-domain Style Recalibration 

FedBCS+ retains the FSR module introduced in FedBCS [24], which recalibrates intermediate feature styles before proto-type construction. 

Motivation for Style-purified Prototype Construction. Ex-isting prototype-based methods assume that same-class fea-tures follow similar distributions across clients and therefore construct prototypes by averaging these features. In medical imaging, however, differences in scanning protocols intro-duce substantial style variations that propagate through network layers and are reflected in the amplitude spec-tra of intermediate features. Direct averaging retains these discrepancies, so representations of the same anatomical structure can differ markedly across institutions, as shown in Sec. 4.4.2. Reliable semantic anchors for cross-client align-ment therefore require style-purified features. We build on the observation that style and content are largely separable in the frequency domain [45], [46], which enables targeted style modulation before prototype construction.

3.2 Decoupled Contextual Prototype Alignment 

Motivation for Decoupled Representations. Existing prototype-based methods [10], [11] typically construct and align prototypes using final-layer features. This approach overlooks the hierarchical information distributed across network layers, which captures both fine-grained tissue details and global anatomical context. Our conference ver-sion [24] introduced CDPA to fuse multi-level encoder and decoder features into a coupled representation. Although this fusion captures richer context, combining deep semantic cues and shallow structural details in one prototype intro-duces representational interference: semantic consensus and structural preservation compete within the shared represen-tation, and averaging coupled prototypes tends to smooth anatomical contours. We examine this effect in Sec. 4.4.3. To address it, DCPA retains this hierarchical fusion and extends CDPA by constructing separate semantic and structural prototypes and aligning them independently.

3.3 Style-purified Semantic Prototype Aggregation 

Motivation for Prototype-guided Aggregation. Fed-BCS [24] uses FedAvg [2] for server-side parameter aggre-gation, with weights proportional to each client’s sample count. S2PA instead derives the aggregation weights from representation differences across clients. Style-purified se-mantic and structural prototypes provide complementary measures of each client’s divergence from the global consen-sus, and this information can therefore guide aggregation.

3.4 Discussion 

Overall Framework. Fig. 2 and Algorithm 1 summarize FedBCS+, which integrates prototype construction, feature alignment, and server aggregation. First, FSR (Sec. 3.1) recalibrates intermediate features in the frequency domain to suppress style bias before prototype construction. Next, DCPA (Sec. 3.2) constructs separate semantic and structural prototypes from the fused multi-level features and aligns them independently. Finally, S2PA (Sec. 3.3) uses their diver-gence from the global consensus to adaptively weight client models and correct aggregation bias. The local training objective is

Communication and Computational Complexity. Each fused stream contributes at most |C| semantic prototypes (one for the background and |C|− 1 for the foreground classes) and |C|− 1 foreground structural prototypes. The per-client prototype payload is therefore O(|R| (2 |C|− 1) d),where |R| = 2 denotes the fused encoder and decoder streams and d is the prototype dimension. Binary seg-mentation requires at most six prototypes; prototypes for missing classes or empty eroded regions are omitted. For local computation, DCPA adds morphological erosion, im-plemented by a single max-pooling pass with complexity 

O(H×W ), and per-pixel InfoNCE against the cluster cen-troids. Both operations are lightweight relative to a standard forward and backward pass. FSR adds a two-dimensional FFT/IFFT pair with complexity O(C×H×W × log( HW )) 

for C channels. On the server, S2PA computes the prototype divergences with complexity O(M |R| (2 |C|− 1) d). Sec. 4.4.4 compares communication and computation costs across methods.

4 EXPERIMENTS 

In this section, we evaluate FedBCS+ on four 2D bench-marks and the 3D FeTS 2022 benchmark. After describing the setup (Sec. 4.1), we compare FedBCS+ with state-of-the-art methods (Sec. 4.2), analyze the contribution of each component (Sec. 4.3), and examine its training dynamics, style separation, prototype entanglement, overhead, and theoretical implications (Sec. 4.4). 

4.1 Experimental Setup 

Datasets. We evaluate four heterogeneous 2D federated segmentation tasks, denoted Histology, Prostate, BUS, and Polyp. Histology follows the four-client nuclei segmen-tation setting of our conference version [24], compris-ing TCIA [49], CRC, KIRC [50], and TNBC [51]. Prostate comprises six clients for T2-weighted MRI segmentation, drawn from NCI-ISBI 2013, I2CVB, and PROMISE12 [52], [53], [54]. BUS comprises four clients for breast lesion segmentation in ultrasound: the Breast Ultrasound Image Database from QAMEBI (BUID), BUSI, BUS UC, and BUS-UCLM [55], [56], [57], [58]. Polyp comprises five clients: the CVC-EndoSceneStill subset commonly referred to as CVC-300 [59], CVC-ClinicDB (ClinicDB) [60], CVC-ColonDB (ColonDB) [61], the EndoTect 2020 segmentation test set (EndoTect) [62], and ETIS-LaribPolypDB (ETIS) [63]. We additionally evaluate 3D brain tumor segmentation using nine institutional partitions from the Federated Tumor Seg-mentation (FeTS) 2022 data release [64], [65]. Table 1 lists the client IDs, imaging modalities, and segmentation tasks. Fig. 3 quantifies heterogeneity in target size using the fore-ground ratio, and Fig. 4 shows an example input from each benchmark. Client datasets range from 50 to 811 images (22 to 511 volumes for FeTS 2022) and differ substantially in their foreground-ratio distributions. 

Evaluation Metrics and Protocol. We use the Dice score, ex-pressed as a percentage, to measure region overlap, and the standard deviation σDice of the per-client mean Dice scores to measure cross-client consistency. The main experiments on the four 2D benchmarks use five-fold cross-validation: each client’s dataset is randomly partitioned into five folds with a fixed seed, the same fold index is held out for testing on all clients, and we report the mean and standard deviation over the five runs. The 3D FeTS 2022 and leave-one-site-out (LOSO) experiments use the first split. In LOSO, which evaluates generalization to unseen domains, one client is excluded from federated training and evaluated without fine-tuning at the round with the highest mean Dice over the remaining clients.

4.2 Comparison with State-of-the-art Methods 

Table 3 compares performance on the four 2D bench-marks under five-fold cross-validation. FedBCS+ achieves the highest mean Dice scores of 75.47%, 82.45%, 79.22%, and 80.78% on Histology, Prostate, BUS, and Polyp, respectively. In the additional 3D evaluation on FeTS 2022, FedBCS+ also achieves the highest mean Dice score of 85.30%, as shown in Table 4. It outperforms the conference version, FedBCS, on all five benchmarks. 

Segmentation Accuracy. As shown in Table 3, FedBCS+ im-proves over FedAvg by 4.33, 3.31, 2.16, and 3.26 Dice points on the four main benchmarks, respectively. It also improves Dice by 1.03 points on 3D FeTS 2022 (Table 4). General FL methods, including FedAvg, MOON, and HarmoFL, lack explicit cross-client prototype alignment. Prototype-based methods, including FedProto, FPL, and FedPLVM, align client representations but rely on single-layer features and conventional aggregation. FedBCS+ targets both limitations by combining the multi-level contextual alignment of DCPA with the prototype-guided weighting of S2PA. The qualita-tive examples in Fig. 4 are consistent with the quantitative results, with fewer missed regions and false positives for FedBCS+. 

Cross-client Consistency and Generalization. FedBCS+ also improves performance consistency across clients. Rel-ative to FedAvg, σDice decreases on all four 2D benchmarks: from 4.20 to 3.51 on Histology, 4.62 to 3.52 on Prostate, 8.39 to 6.56 on BUS, and 10.47 to 7.71 on Polyp. FedBCS+ attains the lowest σDice among all methods on BUS and Polyp and the third lowest on Histology and Prostate. This agrees with the design of S2PA, which gives more weight to clients that are under-represented in the global consensus. Table 5 reports the LOSO results on Histology. FedBCS+ achieves the highest average Dice score of 69.97%, compared with 67.92% for FedBCS and at most 67.14% for the other methods in Table 5. These results indicate better transfer to unseen imaging domains. 

Comparison with the Conference Version. FedBCS+ ex-tends FedBCS in two respects. (1) Decoupled proto-type alignment. DCPA replaces coupled prototype align-ment with separate semantic and structural branches, so that regional and contour cues are aligned separately. In the standalone alignment comparison on Histology (Sec. 4.3.2), this change increases Dice from 71.50% to 72.23%. (2) Adaptive parameter aggregation. Weighting clients by their semantic–structural prototype divergence provides the largest standalone gain of 2.67 Dice points in Table 6. Together, these extensions yield gains over FedBCS ranging from 0.70 Dice points on Prostate to 1.83 points on Histology, with 1.10 points on FeTS 2022. 

Feature Separability. Fig. 5 visualizes pixel-level fore-ground and background encoder features using t-SNE [71] to examine the representations learned by FedBCS+. Com-pared with FedAvg, FedBCS+ produces more separable features on every benchmark. The silhouette score rises most on Histology (from 0.20 to 0.61) and Polyp (from 0.16 to 0.51), and least on FeTS 2022 (from 0.78 to 0.92), where the FedAvg features are already well separated.

4.3.2 Fine-grained Analysis of DCPA 

To examine the role of each prototype type in DCPA, we compare alignment using semantic (center) prototypes, structural (boundary) prototypes, and both types jointly. Table 7 shows that semantic-only and structural-only align-ment achieve 70.87% and 70.30% Dice, respectively. Align-ing both types jointly achieves the highest score of 72.23%, as the two types capture complementary cues, namely re-gional information and contours. Joint alignment also out-performs the coupled CDPA prototype (71.50%), so separate semantic and structural branches guide alignment better than a shared prototype.

Computation. On the shared UNet backbone (Table 2), FSR is the only component that increases the client model size. It adds 902 parameters, increasing the total from 8,266,626 to 8,267,528, or by 0.01%. Each of the three FSR blocks contains a per-channel affine pair and two mixing logits. 5 CONCLUSION 

We have presented FedBCS+, a federated learning frame-work for heterogeneous medical image segmentation that addresses incomplete contextual representations and lay-erwise style and aggregation biases. FSR recalibrates the amplitude spectra of intermediate features to obtain style-purified prototypes, DCPA constructs separate semantic and structural prototypes from multi-level features and aligns them independently, and S2PA converts each client’s pro-totype divergence from the global consensus into adaptive aggregation weights. Experiments on five heterogeneous benchmarks spanning histopathology, MRI, ultrasound, and colonoscopy show that FedBCS+ achieves the highest mean Dice among the compared methods, with gains of up to 4.33 Dice points over FedAvg, and reduces cross-client perfor-mance variation relative to FedAvg on all four 2D bench-marks. The convergence analysis further characterizes how prototype-guided aggregation and decoupled alignment en-ter the optimization bound. The current framework assumes a shared network architecture and has been evaluated with up to nine clients; future work will extend prototype-guided aggregation to heterogeneous architectures, larger federa-tions, and partial client participation.
